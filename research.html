Research Statement

Chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension (CTEPH) is in part caused by dysregulated vascular signaling focused on the calcitonin-like receptor (CLR), whose pharmacology is altered through its association with differential receptor activity-modifying proteins (RAMP1, RAMP2, or RAMP3); thus, a single receptor behaves in distinct manners dependent on the accessory protein bound to it. Prior work has established that RAMP association determines CLR ligand selectivity and downstream bias, with the CLR-RAMP2 complex forming the canonical adrenomedullin receptor linked to pulmonary vascular protection. As such, RAMP type, rather than the ligand alone, can determine which pathway a given complex favors. Consequently, because this bias has been characterized among G protein pathways and not directly between G protein and β-arrestin signaling, my current and future research aims to determine how a specific RAMP can alter the extent and distribution of G protein versus β-arrestin signaling following adrenomedullin stimulation of CLR. Therefore, characterizing and further clarifying this relationship could help to identify which CLR–RAMP complex to target for biased agonist development, offering a route to pulmonary vasculoprotective therapies for CTEPH patients who remain symptomatic after surgical or anticoagulant management.
