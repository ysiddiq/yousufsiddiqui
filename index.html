About Me

Hello, I’m Yousuf Siddiqui, a first-year Pre-Business Administration major and Chemistry minor on the pre-med track at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill. I’m especially interested in the responses induced in the specific signaling pathways of certain cells upon G-protein-coupled receptor binding and how understanding them can help us identify and create new drugs to promote disease prevention and treatment.

My interest in medicine and patient care began at a young age. Growing up around my father and other family members who are medical professionals, I was fortunate to see firsthand the meaningful impact healthcare can have on people's lives. Working as an ophthalmic assistant in my father's practice further confirmed this interest. I saw the trust his patients placed in him, his genuine desire to improve their quality of life, and the love and respect they returned to him. The potential for such an impact has reminded me of how important access to care is in shaping a person's life, and how unevenly that access is distributed. I have been given opportunities in education and training that many people around the world have not, and I feel a responsibility to use them to serve communities where quality care isn’t accessible.

In December of 2024, I traveled to Rawalpindi, Pakistan, where I was privileged to spend some time at Al-Shifa Eye Hospital, where nearly two-thirds of patients receive care free of charge. I was given access to observe several surgeries and was told that the hospital conducts over 200 surgeries a day, restoring vision to people who might otherwise never see clearly again. Upon returning, what struck me most was the realization that there is a lot more to the world than what can be seen at face value. Seeing the sad realities of the living conditions of the people there has given me a true appreciation for all of the privileges I have been given. That is why, upon returning, I initiated an eyeglass collection drive in the Triangle area to send used eyeglasses to their 5 hospitals serving low-income communities abroad.

My passion for serving others translated to my work on Student Government and Honor Council throughout High School, where I served as class president and council chair. I organized class wide events like charity basketball and led council hearings for students convicted of breaching the honor system. These experiences taught me how to organize and communicate ideas clearly, speak with confidence when leading meetings, and work with an adherence to ethics, skills that would assist me in caring for patients and collaborating with medical teams.
